Pilgrimage and yatra packing list

A yatra usually means long walks or queues, early mornings, crowds, simple food and often elders in the group. Pack for comfort on your feet, for modest dress at the shrine, and for the health of the oldest and youngest travellers.

Tips for this kind of trip

  • Decide a meeting point at every stop, and what each person should do if separated. In big crowds phones often do not connect.
  • Many shrines do not allow phones, cameras, leather items or bags inside, and have their own dress rules. Check the shrine's official website or notice board before you go, and use only the official cloakroom.
  • Register or book darshan, accommodation and any required passes only on the official website of the shrine board or the government. Fake booking sites are common around popular yatras.
  • For high-altitude yatras, a medical fitness certificate may be compulsory, and the authorities publish health advisories. Read them, and take elders for a check-up beforehand.
  • Walk at the pace of the slowest member, rest often and drink water even when it is cold.
  • Carry change and small notes for offerings and cloakrooms, and keep the main cash elsewhere.
  • Start early. Queues are shortest and the day is coolest at dawn.

Questions

What should elders carry on a yatra?

Their regular medicines with prescriptions, a card with name and phone numbers, comfortable footwear, a walking stick, warm clothing and glucose or snacks.

Can I carry my phone inside the temple?

It depends on the shrine. Many do not allow phones or cameras inside and provide lockers. Check the shrine's own rules before you travel.
