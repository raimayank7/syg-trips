Travel adapter finder

Pick where you are from and where you are going.

How the answer is worked out

The finder compares the plug types listed for the two countries. If they share a type, a plug from home fits there; if the two-pin type C plug is involved, it also fits type E and F sockets. If nothing matches, you need an adapter for the destination type. It also compares voltage: a gap of more than about 20 volts, such as 230 V to 120 V, means a single-voltage appliance needs a converter, while a charger marked 100-240 V does not. Frequency is compared last, and only matters for clocks and motors.

The result is a guide built from the IEC list and the rules above, not a safety certificate. Always read the label of your own device and check with your hotel for high-power appliances.

Check before you travel. Plug, voltage and frequency data come from the IEC World Plugs list as archived on 2 June 2024. Standards and wiring change, and hotels often have extra sockets or none. Check your device label and ask your hotel or airline for anything that matters, such as medical equipment.
