    The short answer for most trips

    Three things decide whether your devices will work abroad: the plug shape, the voltage and the frequency. The plug shape is the easy one: a travel adapter solves it. The voltage is the one that can cause damage: India uses 230 volts, and countries such as the United States, Canada, Japan and Mexico use around 100 to 127 volts. A phone or laptop charger with "100-240 V" printed on it works on both. A hair dryer, kettle or iron marked "230 V" only does not, and needs a converter or a local replacement.

    This site gives you the plug types, voltage and frequency for each country from the IEC World Plugs list, then explains in plain words what that means when you start from India. It does not replace the label on your device or the advice of your hotel. If you travel with medical equipment, check with the maker and the airline before you go.

    Check before you travel. Plug, voltage and frequency data come from the IEC World Plugs list as archived on 2 June 2024. Standards and wiring change, and hotels often have extra sockets or none. Check your device label and ask your hotel or airline for anything that matters, such as medical equipment.
