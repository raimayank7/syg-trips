Travel adapter or voltage converter: which do you need?

An adapter changes the shape of the plug. A converter changes the voltage. Most travellers need only the first.

Two different problems

When you plug something into a foreign socket, two separate things must be right. The plug has to fit into the socket, and the voltage and frequency coming out of the socket have to suit the device. A plug adapter solves the first problem only. It is a small block of plastic with metal pins on one side and holes on the other, and it does nothing to the electricity. A voltage converter, or transformer, solves the second problem: it changes the voltage, and it is a heavy, expensive piece of equipment that has to be rated for the power of what you plug into it.

People mix them up because many "travel adapters" are sold with a voltage warning in tiny print. The safe habit is to ask two questions in order: does it fit, and does the label on my device say it accepts this voltage?

Do you need an adapter?

You need an adapter when the plug on your device does not fit the socket in the country you are visiting. Indian homes mainly use plug types C, D and M. If a country lists one of these, your plug usually fits. If it lists only other types, such as A and B in North America, G in Britain or I in Australia, you need an adapter for that type. The country pages on this site list the types for each country.

The small two-pin type C plug is the traveller's friend: it fits the sockets of types C, E and F, which together cover most of continental Europe. A three-pin Indian plug, such as on an iron, does not fit those sockets and needs an adapter.

A universal adapter has sliding pins or several plug shapes and covers every type. If you travel to more than one country, it is the simplest thing to carry. A single-country adapter is smaller and often cheaper, but useless on the next trip.

Do you need a converter?

Look at your device's label. On the power brick of a phone, tablet, laptop or camera charger you will normally find a line like "Input: 100-240 V, 50-60 Hz". That means the device accepts any supply in that range, so it works anywhere in the world with just a plug adapter, and a converter would be a waste.

If the label says only "230 V" or "220-240 V", the device is built for the Indian supply. In a country on 110 or 120 volts, it will run at about half the power it expects, if it runs at all. Heating appliances will heat slowly and motors may overheat. In that case you either need a converter that is rated for the appliance's power, or you should buy a cheap local appliance, or use the hotel's own.

Going the other way, taking a 110 V-only device from North America to India without a converter risks burning it out.

Power rating

A converter is rated in watts. Add up the power of whatever you plan to plug into it and choose a converter with a rating comfortably higher. Heating appliances, such as hair dryers, kettles and irons, draw a lot of power and need large converters; a phone charger draws almost none. Many people find it simpler to leave heating appliances at home.

Quick decision

  1. Check the label of every device you plan to take.
  2. If it says 100-240 V: bring a plug adapter only.
  3. If it says 230 V only and the destination is on 220-240 V: bring a plug adapter only.
  4. If it says 230 V only and the destination is on 100-127 V: use a converter rated for its power, or do not take the device.
Is a transformer the same as a converter?

In everyday travel talk, yes, roughly. A transformer changes voltage and is usually for steady use. A converter often has a mode for heating appliances. The label on the product tells you what it is designed for.

Can I use my adapter for a hair dryer?

An adapter only changes the plug shape. A hair dryer also needs the right voltage and a high power capacity, so check both the adapter's power rating and the dryer's label.

Check before you travel. Plug, voltage and frequency data come from the IEC World Plugs list as archived on 2 June 2024. Standards and wiring change, and hotels often have extra sockets or none. Check your device label and ask your hotel or airline for anything that matters, such as medical equipment.
