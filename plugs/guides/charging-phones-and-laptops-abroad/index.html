Charging phones and laptops abroad

Charging abroad is easy once you have one good adapter and know your charger labels.

The short list

Most travellers need only four things: a plug adapter for the destination, the chargers they normally use, a USB cable for each device, and a spare power bank. The sections below explain each.

Choose the adapter

Look up the country on this site to see which plug types it uses. For one destination, a simple adapter for that type will do. For several destinations, or if you do not know which type the hotel has, a universal adapter with sliding pins covers every type. Choose one with a built-in fuse or surge protection if you will plug in a laptop. Some adapters have USB sockets, which lets you charge a phone without carrying a separate charger.

Carry fewer chargers

Every extra charger needs a socket. A single multi-port USB charger, marked 100-240 V, can charge a phone, a tablet and earbuds at once from one socket. Check that its total output is enough for a laptop if you hope to charge that too; many laptops need a charger of their own.

Check each label

Before you pack, read the input line on each charger: it should say "100-240V". See how to read the label. If it does, no converter is needed anywhere.

Power banks

A power bank is a battery, not a mains device, so voltage and plug do not matter for it. Charge it from a socket using a 100-240 V charger. Airlines have rules about power banks, which must go in the cabin bag and not in checked luggage; check your airline's current rule before you fly.

Where the sockets are

Hotel rooms may have few sockets, and they are not always near the bed. A short extension cable or a small power strip, plugged into the adapter, solves this. Keep a USB cable long enough to reach the bed. Airports and trains usually have charging points, but they may be crowded or out of order, so start the day with devices charged.

Airport checklist

  1. Plug adapter in the cabin bag, not the checked bag.
  2. Chargers and cables in a small pouch, so that they come out in one go at security.
  3. Power bank charged the night before, in the cabin bag.
  4. Laptop charger packed even if the battery is full.

When something goes wrong

If an adapter is warm, the plug is loose, or a device does not charge, unplug it. A loose adapter can cause sparks. Swap to another socket, or use a different adapter. Do not push a plug into a socket with force.

Do I need a converter for a laptop?

Almost never. Laptop chargers are nearly always 100-240 V. Check the label.

Can I charge two phones from one adapter?

Yes, with a multi-port USB charger or a power strip, as long as the adapter's power rating covers the total.

Check before you travel. Plug, voltage and frequency data come from the IEC World Plugs list as archived on 2 June 2024. Standards and wiring change, and hotels often have extra sockets or none. Check your device label and ask your hotel or airline for anything that matters, such as medical equipment.
