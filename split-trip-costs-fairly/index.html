How to split trip costs fairly

Fair does not always mean equal. Agree a few simple rules before the trip, and money stops being a topic during it.

Agree before you go

The awkward conversations all come from rules invented after the money was spent. Ten minutes before the trip, settle four questions:

  1. What is shared? Usually travel taken together, shared rooms, common meals, the hired vehicle, guides and group activities.
  2. What is personal? Shopping, gifts, individual treatments or adventure rides, phone recharges, personal snacks.
  3. How are unequal things handled? See the cases below.
  4. Who records, and when do we settle? One person, daily entries, settlement within a week of return.

The common methods

MethodHow it worksSuits
Equal splitTotal shared cost divided by the number of peopleFriends with similar habits and budgets
Per expenseEach expense is split among only those who took partMixed groups where not everyone does everything
Per family unitEach family pays a share by its number of members, or a fixed shareFamily trips
Common kittyEveryone puts in the same amount up front; shared costs are paid from itSmall daily spends; trips with one clear organiser
Pay your ownEach person pays their own tickets and room; only truly joint costs are sharedColleagues, large groups, very different budgets

Most groups do best with a mix: pay your own for travel and rooms, a kitty for small things, and per expense for everything else. The cost splitter handles per-expense sharing: each entry can be shared by everyone or by a chosen few.

The cases that cause friction

Rooms. Two people sharing a room each pay half of it. Someone who takes a single room pays for the whole room. If rooms differ in size or view, either rotate or let the better room pay a little more.

Children. Common practice: infants are not counted; small children count as half for food and shared transport, and by actual cost where tickets are priced separately. Decide the ages before the trip, not at the restaurant.

Alcohol and non-vegetarian food. Ask for these on a separate bill, shared only by those who had them. This single habit prevents most resentment in mixed groups.

Different appetites for activities. The ropeway, the scuba dive, the spa: paid by those who go.

The car owner. When one person's car is used, the others share fuel and tolls. Many groups also cover the owner's share of fuel, or a little extra, for the wear on the car. Use the fuel cost calculator.

The driver among friends. The person who drives all day is doing work. Covering their meals on the road, or their share of fuel, is a fair thank-you.

Different budgets. If one person wants a costlier hotel, they can book it themselves and join the group for the day, or the group settles on the modest choice. Never split an upgrade among people who did not want it.

Couples. Two people, two shares. A couple is not one unit unless everyone is in couples.

Late joiners and early leavers. They share only the days and expenses they were present for. Enter those expenses with only the people present ticked.

Advances and cancellations. Whoever cancels bears the cancellation charge on their own booking, and their part of anything booked jointly that cannot be refunded.

Running a kitty

  1. Everyone gives the same amount to one person at the start.
  2. Small shared costs are paid from it: tea, water, parking, tips, entry tickets.
  3. The holder notes each spend, or simply counts what is left each evening.
  4. Top up equally when it runs low.
  5. At the end, the remainder is returned equally, or spent on the last meal.

The kitty removes hundreds of tiny entries from the record. Keep big items out of it and in the splitter.

Keeping the record

  • Enter each expense the same day, with a word for what it was.
  • Photograph bills.
  • Record who actually paid, even when one person paid by card for everyone and was partly repaid in cash at the table. Simplest is to avoid part-repayments during the trip and settle everything once at the end.
  • Share the running summary every two or three days, so that no figure is a surprise.

Settling up

  • Use the splitter's "who pays whom" list: it reduces everything to a few payments.
  • Round to the nearest ten rupees by agreement.
  • Pay within a week. The longer it waits, the more it rankles.
  • If someone is short of money that month, let them say so, and agree a date.

When you are paid by UPI, you do nothing but check that the money has arrived. Entering your PIN or scanning a code is never needed to receive.

When fairness and friendship pull apart

Small differences are not worth a friendship. If the count is out by a few rupees, let it go. If one person repeatedly does not pay, do not lecture: next time, use "pay your own" bookings and a kitty collected in advance.

Should costs be split equally or by usage?

Equally when habits are similar; by usage, expense by expense, when some people skip meals, drinks or activities. Decide before the trip.

How are children counted when splitting costs?

Commonly, infants are not counted and small children count as half, except where their tickets or meals are priced separately. Agree the rule in advance.
