How to plan a trip, step by step

A good plan is short, honest about time and has room to breathe. Here is a method that works for most trips.

Start with the people and the dates

Before you look at places, answer three questions. Who is going? A trip for two adults who like to walk is different from a trip with small children, elders, or a mix of ages and tastes. When can you go, and for how long? The dates decide the season, the prices and the weather. What is the point of the trip? Rest, adventure, family time, a festival, a pilgrimage, a wedding: say it in one line. When you cannot agree on the places, the one-line purpose is what you go back to.

Choose the place with the season in mind

Look at the weather for your dates, the usual crowds, and the time you will lose in travelling. A place that is perfect in one month can be miserable in another because of heat, rain, cold or crowds. Look for official information on entry rules, permits and closures for your dates, and for the weather of the month. Our other sections show climate and visa facilities for many places.

Fix the big pieces first

Three things shape the whole trip: getting there and back, where you sleep and anything that is fixed in time, such as a flight, a festival, a ticketed event or a booked tour. Put these into the plan first, as the frame. Everything else fits between them. Book the pieces that sell out early, but check the cancellation terms before you pay.

Add the days, one at a time

For each day, write down what you want to do in the order you will do it, with times where times matter. Then do two checks. First, time: add up the travel between places and the time at each, and see if it fits in the day with meals and rest. Most first drafts are too full. Second, energy: a day with a long journey in the morning should have a light afternoon. Our guide to pacing days goes through this.

Add the money

Put a cost on each item as you plan, even a rough one, and let the tool add them up. A budget made at the end is a guess; a budget built from the plan is a tool. Include the things that are easy to forget: local transport, tips, entry fees, bags, a SIM or data, and something for emergencies. See how to budget a trip.

Add the checklist

Write a list of everything that must happen before you leave: documents, bookings, money, medicines, chargers, a way to reach each other, someone at home who knows your plan. Tick the items off. The tool has a button that adds the usual ones to start.

Share it and keep a copy

Send the plan to the people who are coming and to one person who is not, so that someone at home knows where you will be. Keep a copy that works without the internet: a printout and a screenshot are both good.

Review, then loosen

Read the whole plan once more, as if you were someone else. Cut one thing from every day that has too much. Check that nothing important is on the first morning after a long journey or on the last day before a flight. A plan with a little slack in it survives a delay; a tight plan breaks at the first one.

How far ahead should I plan?

Far enough to book the things that run out or rise in price. For many trips that is weeks or months. Keep the rest flexible.

Should I plan every hour?

No. Plan the fixed points and the main thing of each day. Leave the rest open.
