Itinerary mistakes and how to avoid them

Most trip problems come from a handful of planning mistakes. Check your plan against this list.

1. Too much in a day

The most common mistake. The cure is the pacing rule: one main thing in the morning, one in the afternoon, and an easy evening. See how to pace the days. If a day has more than two big things, cut one.

2. Forgetting how long travel takes

Maps give the time of an ideal drive. In real life there is traffic, a wrong turn, a queue at the gate, a toilet stop, and a wait for a taxi. Add a margin of at least a quarter to the time the map gives, and more on mountain roads or in big cities. Count the journey from door to door, not only the time in the train or bus.

3. Ignoring opening days and times

Many places close one day a week or on public holidays, and many have a last entry time before closing. Check the official page of every place that matters to you, for the specific date. The planner has a note field: write the opening hours you found and the date you checked.

4. Not booking what sells out

Some tickets, tours, permits and rooms sell out well in advance, particularly in the high season or around festivals. If a place is the point of the trip, book it first, then build the plan around it. Check the cancellation terms before you pay.

5. Arriving late and starting early

A late arrival followed by an early start the next morning makes the first day miserable. If you arrive late, make the next morning slow. If you must start early, go to bed early the night before.

6. No buffer before a departure

Leave a buffer before every flight or train. Delays on the way, queues at security and long walks in stations eat time. On the last day, do not plan a big visit if your departure is in the afternoon.

7. No rest and no meals

A plan with no meals and no rest assumes that everyone can go without. They cannot. Put both into the plan.

8. Ignoring the weather and the season

A place can be beautiful in one month and unpleasant in another because of heat, rain, cold or crowds. Look at the weather for your dates and plan indoor alternatives for wet days. Pack for the weather, not for your hopes.

9. Planning for yourself, not the group

If you travel with children, elders, or people with different tastes, plan for the slowest and the most easily tired. See planning with a group.

10. A budget that stops at tickets

Local transport, tips, fees, SIM cards, shopping and insurance are easy to forget and they add up. See how to budget a trip.

11. No copy that works without the internet

Phones run out of power and networks fail. Keep a printout or a screenshot of the plan, the tickets, the bookings, the addresses and the phone numbers. Keep documents in two places.

12. Telling nobody

Tell someone at home where you will be and when. Share the plan with them, and agree when you will check in.

Your review checklist

  • Does any day have more than two big things?
  • Is the travel time padded?
  • Are opening days and times checked?
  • Are the scarce items booked?
  • Is there a buffer before departures?
  • Are meals and rest in the plan?
  • Is the weather considered?
  • Does the plan suit the slowest person?
  • Is the budget complete and does it have a buffer?
  • Do you have a copy offline, and does someone at home have the plan?
Which mistake matters most?

Overfilling days. It makes every other problem worse.

How often should I review the plan?

Once when you finish it, once a few days before you leave, and each evening during the trip.
