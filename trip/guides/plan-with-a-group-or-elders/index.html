Planning with a group, children or elders

The trip that works for everyone is planned around the people with the least energy and the fewest choices.

Agree the purpose first

When several people travel together, they usually want different things: one wants to see everything, one wants to rest, one wants to eat, one wants to shop. Before you pick places, talk about it, and write down in one line what the trip is for. When two people disagree about a day, the line settles it. If the purpose is a family gathering, a day of sightseeing may matter less than a long lunch together.

Plan for the slowest and the most easily tired

Plan the pace for the slowest walker, the youngest child and the person who tires first. A day that suits them will not be too slow for anyone, but a day that suits the fastest will be too much for others. Build in rests, shorter walking, more bathroom stops and an early night. If a day is a stretch for someone, give them a choice to skip a part and rest while others go on.

Travelling with children

Children need regular meals, snacks, water, a place to run, and rest. Plan fewer things per day than you would for adults, put the main thing in the morning when they are fresh, and keep a quiet hour in the afternoon. Carry what keeps them calm on the way: games, books, snacks. Keep a small bag with spare clothes, wet wipes and any medicines. Travel times with children are longer than the map says.

Travelling with elders

Check access before you book: stairs, steep paths, distance from the car to the entrance, seating, and lifts. Ask whether a wheelchair or a chair for carrying is available at places with a lot of walking, and book it in advance if you need it. Plan rest in the middle of the day, particularly in heat. Carry medicines in the hand bag, with copies of the prescriptions, and enough for delays. Keep a list of the doctors' names and a family contact in the plan. Do not plan an early start after a late arrival.

Share the plan with everyone

Send the whole plan to everyone before the trip, on WhatsApp or as a printout, so that everyone knows where to be and when. The planner can copy the plan as text or as a link. Ask people to say now if something is a problem, not on the day. Share the plan with someone who is not travelling.

Decide the money in advance

Money causes more quarrels on group trips than anything else. Agree how costs are shared: equally, or by what each person uses. Agree what is included and what each pays for themselves. Decide who books what, and who pays upfront. Keep a simple record of shared costs as the trip goes on, so that settling up is easy at the end. Our cost splitter works out the fewest payments.

Keep together and keep in touch

Agree a meeting point and a time at every busy place, and what to do if someone is lost: stay where you are, call, and meet at the meeting point. Make sure every adult has the phone numbers of the others and the address of the hotel written down or saved. For children, write a phone number on a card in the pocket.

Give each person something

On a trip with different tastes, give each person a part of the plan that is theirs: a morning of shopping for one, a museum for another, a long rest for a third. The trip feels fairer, and nobody feels dragged along.

Review after each day

At dinner, ask what worked and what did not, and change tomorrow's plan. People are more willing to follow a plan that they can change.

What if people cannot agree on a place?

Return to the one-line purpose. If it does not decide the question, choose a place that has something for everyone, or split the trip.

How do I deal with someone who wants to go off on their own?

Agree a meeting time and place and a way to reach each other. Let them go.
