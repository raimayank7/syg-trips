How to budget a trip

A budget built from the plan is a tool. A budget made at the end is a guess.

Build it from the plan

The most reliable way to budget a trip is to put a cost on every item of the plan as you add it. A rough figure is better than none. When the plan is full, the total is your budget. The planner on this site does the adding and shows the total for each day, for each kind of spending and for each person.

The five kinds of spending

The planner groups costs into five kinds so that you can see where the money goes:

  • Travel: tickets, fuel, tolls, taxis, local transport, baggage fees.
  • Stay: hotels, homestays, camping, and any taxes or fees on them.
  • Food: meals, snacks, coffee, water.
  • Sights: entry tickets, guides, tours, activities, camera fees.
  • Other: everything else, such as shopping, tips, a SIM card or data, insurance, laundry, and gifts.

Looking at the shares tells you what to cut if the total is too high. If travel is half the budget, a closer place or a different route may save more than skipping the museum.

Costs people forget

  • Getting to and from the station or airport at each end.
  • Local transport inside the place: it adds up.
  • Tips and service charges where they are customary.
  • Entry fees and camera fees, which can be separate from the main ticket.
  • Luggage fees, parking and tolls on a road trip.
  • A phone data plan or SIM for a foreign trip.
  • Travel insurance.
  • Small shopping: gifts, souvenirs, things you forgot to pack.
  • Currency exchange costs and card fees on foreign trips.

Write a line for each of these, even if the figure is zero, so that you have thought about it.

Add a buffer

Prices change, plans change, and things go wrong. Add a buffer to the total. Ten to twenty per cent is a common rule of thumb, and more for a long or remote trip, or one with children or elders. The buffer is not for extra spending; it is for the unexpected.

Cash, cards and a spare

For a trip abroad, take more than one way of paying: a card, another card from a different bank, and a little cash in the local currency. Tell your bank you are travelling if the bank asks for that. Keep a spare card and some cash separate from the rest, in case a wallet is lost.

Sharing the total

With a group, decide how to share costs before the trip, not after. Options: split everything equally, or have each person pay for their own items and settle up at the end. For a group trip with many small payments, our cost splitter works out who owes whom, with the fewest payments. In the planner, the number of people gives a per-person figure, which is the total divided equally.

Check prices on official sources

This site gives no prices because prices change and differ by season and operator. When you fill in costs, take them from the booking page, the official ticket site or a written quote, and note the date you checked. Bookings that you pay later can cost more than you expect.

Review the budget

Look at the total, the kind with the biggest share, and the per-person figure. Ask whether you are happy with each. If not, change the plan, not the figures. A budget that fits the plan on paper but not in your pocket will fail on the trip.

How much should I budget per day?

There is no universal figure. Build it from your own plan.

Should I include the cost of getting there?

Yes. It is often the largest single cost.
