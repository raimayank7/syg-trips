Travel safety: money, phones, transport and tourist traps

Most travel trouble is small and preventable: a lost phone, an overcharging taxi, an upset stomach. A few habits remove nearly all of it.

Money and documents

  • Split it up. One card and the day's cash in the wallet; a second card and reserve cash elsewhere, in an inner pocket, a money belt or the room safe. Never everything in one bag.
  • Copies. Photograph identity cards, tickets and bookings, and email them to yourself. Keep paper copies apart from the originals.
  • In crowds, wear the backpack on the front and keep the phone in a front pocket or zipped bag, not in a back pocket or an open handbag.
  • ATMs: use machines inside bank branches or busy, well-lit places; cover the keypad; refuse "help" from bystanders.
  • Cards: do not let a card leave your sight in a restaurant or shop. Check the amount on the machine before entering the PIN.
  • UPI: you never enter a PIN or scan a code to *receive* money. Check the name shown before paying.

Transport

  • Taxis and autos: use the official prepaid counter at stations and airports, an app cab from the marked pick-up point, or a vehicle arranged by your hotel. Agree the fare or insist on the meter before getting in. Note the vehicle number and share your ride with someone.
  • Ignore touts who approach inside stations and airports saying your hotel is closed, your train is cancelled or the road is blocked. Check for yourself at the official enquiry counter or by phoning the hotel.
  • Night travel: prefer arriving in daylight in an unfamiliar town. If you must arrive late, arrange the pick-up in advance.
  • Buses and trains: keep the small bag with valuables on your lap or under your head, chain larger bags, and do not accept food or drink from strangers.
  • Two-wheeler hire: wear the helmet, check brakes and lights, photograph existing scratches before taking it, and carry your licence.
  • Seat belts, front and back, on every road journey.

Where you stay

  • Book through the hotel's own site or a known platform, and read recent reviews. Pay to an account in the business's name.
  • On arrival, check that the door locks and bolts from inside, and note the nearest staircase.
  • Use the room safe or the reception's locker for passports and spare cash.
  • Do not open the door to unexpected "staff"; phone reception to check.
  • Keep the hotel's card with its name, address and phone in your pocket, in the local language where you cannot speak it.

Food and water

  • Drink sealed bottled water, or boiled or filtered water. Check the seal.
  • Eat where it is busy and the food is cooked fresh and served hot.
  • Be careful with cut fruit, salads, ice and uncovered food, especially in summer and the rains.
  • Wash or sanitise hands before eating.
  • Carry ORS sachets. For a stomach upset, rest, fluids and plain food; see a doctor if there is fever, blood, or it lasts beyond a day or two, and sooner for children and elders.

Personal safety

  • Share your itinerary and live location with someone at home.
  • Dress and behave in keeping with local custom, particularly at religious places.
  • Keep your wits: go easy on alcohol among strangers, and do not leave a drink unattended.
  • Trust your unease. If a place or person feels wrong, leave.
  • Solo travellers: stay in well-reviewed, central places; tell the hotel when to expect you back; avoid lonely stretches after dark.
  • Respect water and heights. Most serious tourist accidents happen at waterfalls, sea rocks, cliff edges and on rented vehicles, often while taking photographs.

If something goes wrong

  • Lost or stolen phone: follow the lost phone checklist: lock it remotely, block the SIM, tell the bank.
  • Lost card: call the bank's number, saved separately from your wallet, and block it.
  • Lost documents: report to the police and get a copy of the report; for a passport abroad, contact the Indian embassy or consulate.
  • Fraud: in India, dial 1930 for financial cyber fraud and report on cybercrime.gov.in.
  • Illness or accident: go to the nearest hospital, and call your insurer's emergency number if you have travel cover.

Keep emergency numbers, the hotel's number and a family contact on paper, in case the phone is the thing that is lost.

Is it safe to carry all my cash in one place?

No. Divide cash and cards between your wallet, an inner pocket or money belt, and your bag or the room safe.

How do I avoid being overcharged by taxis?

Use prepaid counters or app cabs, agree the fare or the meter before you start, and ask your hotel what the usual fare is.
