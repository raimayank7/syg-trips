Hungary in numbers

The highest listed mountain, Geschriebenstein, stands 884 metres (2,900 feet) above sea level and ranks 971 among the 1127 mountains in our list. The 2 mountains listed have a median height of 884 metres. The 3 lakes listed cover 1,034 square kilometres in all (399 square miles), and Lake Balaton ranks 222 of 568 in the world list. The 12 rivers listed add up to about 7,605 kilometres (4,726 miles), and the longest, Danube, is 2,850 kilometres, rank 24 of 776.

14 of the 17 items are shared with at least one other country, for example Geschriebenstein (Austria, Hungary), so their figures describe the whole feature, not only the part inside Hungary.

The mountains of Hungary one by one

  • Geschriebenstein: 884 m (2,900 ft), world rank 971 of 1127. It belongs to the Kőszeg Mountains. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 414 m. It lies at 47.35° north, 16.43° east.
  • Jakab-hegy: 602 m (1,975 ft), world rank 1032 of 1127. It belongs to the Mecsek. It lies at 46.09° north, 18.14° east.

The tallest and the lowest of the 2 mountains listed for Hungary differ by 282 m. None reaches 5,000 m.

The lakes of Hungary one by one

  • Lake Balaton: about 592 km² (229 square miles), world rank 222 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 12 m.
  • Neusiedl Lake: about 315 km² (122 square miles), world rank 306 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 2 m. It is shared with Austria.
  • Lake Tisza: about 127 km² (49 square miles), world rank 438 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 17 m.
