Bosnia and Herzegovina in numbers

The highest listed mountain, Maglić, stands 2,386 metres (7,828 feet) above sea level and ranks 634 among the 1127 mountains in our list. The 6 mountains listed have a median height of 2,088 metres. The 1 lake listed covers 56 square kilometres in all (22 square miles), and Buško Blato ranks 546 of 568 in the world list. The 7 rivers listed add up to about 2,465 kilometres (1,531 miles), and the longest, Sava, is 945 kilometres, rank 218 of 776.

6 of the 14 items are shared with at least one other country, for example Maglić (Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro), so their figures describe the whole feature, not only the part inside Bosnia and Herzegovina.

The mountains of Bosnia and Herzegovina one by one

  • Maglić: 2,386 m (7,828 ft), world rank 634 of 1127. It belongs to the Maglić massif. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 51 m. It lies at 43.28° north, 18.73° east.
  • Čvrsnica: 2,228 m (7,310 ft), world rank 670 of 1127. It belongs to the Dinaric Alps. It lies at 43.62° north, 17.62° east.
  • Treskavica: 2,088 m (6,850 ft), world rank 695 of 1127. It belongs to the Dinaric Alps. It lies at 43.59° north, 18.36° east.
  • Zelengora: 2,014 m (6,608 ft), world rank 717 of 1127. It belongs to the Dinaric Alps. It lies at 43.42° north, 18.49° east.
  • Trebević: 1,629 m (5,344 ft), world rank 815 of 1127. It belongs to the Jahorina. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 343 m. It lies at 43.82° north, 18.45° east.
  • Ozren: 918 m (3,012 ft), world rank 962 of 1127. It belongs to the Dinaric Alps. It lies at 44.59° north, 18.28° east.

The tallest and the lowest of the 6 mountains listed for Bosnia and Herzegovina differ by 1,468 m. None reaches 5,000 m.
