Mountains, lakes and rivers of Uganda

Uganda has 2 mountains, 8 lakes and 4 rivers in our lists. Its highest listed mountain is Mount Elgon at 4,321 metres. Its largest listed lake is Lake Victoria at 68,100 square kilometres. Its longest listed river is Nile at about 6,650 kilometres. Rankings are the world rankings, so a rank of 500 is a rank among all the mountains in our list, not within the country.

Uganda in numbers

The highest listed mountain, Mount Elgon, stands 4,321 metres (14,177 feet) above sea level and ranks 253 among the 1127 mountains in our list. The 2 mountains listed have a median height of 4,321 metres. The 8 lakes listed cover 78,340 square kilometres in all (30,247 square miles), and Lake Victoria ranks 3 of 568 in the world list. The 4 rivers listed add up to about 9,900 kilometres (6,152 miles), and the longest, Nile, is 6,650 kilometres, rank 2 of 776.

9 of the 14 items are shared with at least one other country, for example Mount Elgon (Kenya, Uganda), so their figures describe the whole feature, not only the part inside Uganda.

Heights, areas and lengths are the figures from Wikidata as downloaded on the date given below, converted here to feet and miles. Open any item for its own page with comparisons and a note on how the measurement is made.

The mountains of Uganda one by one

  • Mount Elgon: 4,321 m (14,177 ft), world rank 253 of 1127. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,458 m. It lies at 1.12° north, 34.53° east.
  • Mount Sabyinyo: 3,645 m (11,959 ft), world rank 385 of 1127. It belongs to the Virunga Mountains. It lies at 1.40° south, 29.60° east.

The tallest and the lowest of the 2 mountains listed for Uganda differ by 676 m. None reaches 5,000 m.

The lakes of Uganda one by one

  • Lake Victoria: about 68,100 km² (26,294 square miles), world rank 3 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 83 m. It is shared with Kenya, Tanzania.
  • Lake Albert: about 5,270 km² (2,035 square miles), world rank 41 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 58 m. It is shared with Democratic Republic of the Congo.
  • Lake Edward: about 2,150 km² (830 square miles), world rank 97 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 117 m. It is shared with Democratic Republic of the Congo.
  • Lake Kyoga: about 1,720 km² (664 square miles), world rank 117 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 6 m.
  • Lake Kwania: about 540 km² (208 square miles), world rank 237 of 568.
  • Lake George: about 250 km² (97 square miles), world rank 339 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 7 m.
  • Lake Wamala: about 250 km² (97 square miles), world rank 342 of 568.
  • Lake Bunyonyi: about 60 km² (23 square miles), world rank 534 of 568.

The rivers of Uganda one by one

  • Nile: about 6,650 km (4,132 miles), world rank 2 of 776. It ends at Mediterranean Sea. It is shared with Burundi, Egypt, Ethiopia, Rwanda, South Sudan, Sudan, so only part of this length lies in Uganda.
  • White Nile: about 2,620 km (1,628 miles), world rank 34 of 776. It ends at Nile. It is shared with Democratic Republic of the Congo, Rwanda, South Sudan, Sudan, Tanzania, so only part of this length lies in Uganda.
  • Kagera River: about 400 km (249 miles), world rank 488 of 776. It ends at Lake Victoria. It is shared with Burundi, Rwanda, Tanzania, so only part of this length lies in Uganda.
  • Semliki River: about 230 km (143 miles), world rank 655 of 776. It ends at Lake Albert. It is shared with Democratic Republic of the Congo, so only part of this length lies in Uganda.

About these figures. Heights, areas and lengths come from Wikidata (CC0), downloaded on 14 November 2026. Measurements differ between sources, and rivers and lakes change; where Wikidata holds several values we use the middle one and say so. Treat the numbers as good estimates, not survey results.
