Mongolia in numbers

The highest listed mountain, Hüiten Peak, stands 4,374 metres (14,350 feet) above sea level and ranks 246 among the 1127 mountains in our list. The 3 mountains listed have a median height of 4,008 metres. The 10 lakes listed cover 8,007 square kilometres in all (3,092 square miles), and Khövsgöl Lake ranks 75 of 568 in the world list. The 7 rivers listed add up to about 5,823 kilometres (3,618 miles), and the longest, Kherlen River, is 1,254 kilometres, rank 134 of 776.

6 of the 20 items are shared with at least one other country, for example Hüiten Peak (Mongolia, People's Republic of China), so their figures describe the whole feature, not only the part inside Mongolia.

The mountains of Mongolia one by one

  • Hüiten Peak: 4,374 m (14,350 ft), world rank 246 of 1127. It belongs to the Altai Mountains. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,342 m. It lies at 49.15° north, 87.82° east.
  • Otgontenger: 4,008 m (13,150 ft), world rank 318 of 1127. It belongs to the Khangai Mountains. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,259 m. It lies at 47.64° north, 97.50° east.
  • Burkhan Khaldun: 2,450 m (8,038 ft), world rank 622 of 1127. It belongs to the Khentii Mountains. It lies at 48.78° north, 109.17° east.

The tallest and the lowest of the 3 mountains listed for Mongolia differ by 1,924 m. None reaches 5,000 m.
