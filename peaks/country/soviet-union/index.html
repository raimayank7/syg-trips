The mountains of Soviet Union one by one

  • Lenin Peak: 7,134 m (23,406 ft), world rank 73 of 1127. It belongs to the Trans-Alay Range. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,853 m. It lies at 39.34° north, 72.88° east.
  • Shkhara: 5,193 m (17,038 ft), world rank 181 of 1127. It belongs to the Bezengi Wall. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 1,357 m. It lies at 43.00° north, 43.11° east.

The tallest and the lowest of the 2 mountains listed for Soviet Union differ by 1,941 m. 2 of them are on the 5,000 m line or above.

The rivers of Soviet Union one by one

  • Cheptsa: about 501 km (311 miles), world rank 404 of 776. It ends at Vyatka. It is shared with Russia, Russian Empire, so only part of this length lies in Soviet Union.
  • Upper Angara: about 438 km (272 miles), world rank 460 of 776. It ends at Angara. It is shared with Russia, Russian Empire, so only part of this length lies in Soviet Union.
  • Sviyaga: about 375 km (233 miles), world rank 505 of 776. It ends at Volga. It is shared with Russia, Russian Empire, so only part of this length lies in Soviet Union.
