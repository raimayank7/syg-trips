Mountains, lakes and rivers of Kenya

Kenya in numbers

The highest listed mountain, Mount Kenya, stands 5,199 metres (17,057 feet) above sea level and ranks 180 among the 1127 mountains in our list. The 5 mountains listed have a median height of 2,776 metres. The 8 lakes listed cover 76,507 square kilometres in all (29,540 square miles), and Lake Victoria ranks 3 of 568 in the world list. The 3 rivers listed add up to about 1,585 kilometres (985 miles), and the longest, Tana River, is 800 kilometres, rank 264 of 776.

5 of the 16 items are shared with at least one other country, for example Mount Elgon (Kenya, Uganda), so their figures describe the whole feature, not only the part inside Kenya.

Heights, areas and lengths are the figures from Wikidata as downloaded on the date given below, converted here to feet and miles. Open any item for its own page with comparisons and a note on how the measurement is made.

The mountains of Kenya one by one

  • Mount Kenya: 5,199 m (17,057 ft), world rank 180 of 1127. It belongs to the Mount Kilimanjaro. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 3,825 m. It lies at 0.10° south, 37.20° east.
  • Mount Elgon: 4,321 m (14,177 ft), world rank 253 of 1127. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,458 m. It lies at 1.12° north, 34.53° east.
  • Mount Longonot: 2,776 m (9,108 ft), world rank 541 of 1127. It belongs to the East African Rift. It lies at 0.92° south, 36.46° east.
  • Menengai: 2,278 m (7,474 ft), world rank 656 of 1127. It belongs to the Aberdare Range. It lies at 0.20° south, 36.07° east.
  • Mount Homa: 1,751 m (5,745 ft), world rank 776 of 1127. It lies at 0.38° south, 34.50° east.

The tallest and the lowest of the 5 mountains listed for Kenya differ by 3,448 m. 1 of them are on the 5,000 m line or above.

The lakes of Kenya one by one

  • Lake Victoria: about 68,100 km² (26,294 square miles), world rank 3 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 83 m. It is shared with Tanzania, Uganda.
  • Lake Turkana: about 6,405 km² (2,473 square miles), world rank 30 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 109 m. It is shared with Ethiopia.
  • Lake Chew Bahir: about 1,200 km² (463 square miles), world rank 143 of 568. It is shared with Ethiopia.
  • Lake Magadi: about 200 km² (77 square miles), world rank 380 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 1 m.
  • Lake Nakuru: about 188 km² (73 square miles), world rank 383 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 3 m.
  • Lake Baringo: about 168 km² (65 square miles), world rank 398 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 5 m.
  • Lake Naivasha: about 139 km² (54 square miles), world rank 425 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 6 m.
  • Lake Bogoria: about 107 km² (41 square miles), world rank 458 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 10 m.

The rivers of Kenya one by one

  • Tana River: about 800 km (497 miles), world rank 264 of 776. It ends at Indian Ocean.
  • Mara River: about 395 km (245 miles), world rank 493 of 776. It ends at Lake Victoria. It is shared with Tanzania, so only part of this length lies in Kenya.
  • Athi-Galana-Sabaki River: about 390 km (242 miles), world rank 494 of 776. It ends at Indian Ocean.

About these figures. Heights, areas and lengths come from Wikidata (CC0), downloaded on 14 November 2026. Measurements differ between sources, and rivers and lakes change; where Wikidata holds several values we use the middle one and say so. Treat the numbers as good estimates, not survey results.
