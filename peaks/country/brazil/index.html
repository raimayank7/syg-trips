Brazil in numbers

The highest listed mountain, Pico da Neblina, stands 2,994 metres (9,823 feet) above sea level and ranks 499 among the 1127 mountains in our list. The 5 mountains listed have a median height of 2,810 metres. The 2 lakes listed cover 13,994 square kilometres in all (5,403 square miles), and Lagoa dos Patos ranks 19 of 568 in the world list. The 26 rivers listed add up to about 53,591 kilometres (33,300 miles), and the longest, Amazon River, is 6,800 kilometres, rank 1 of 776.

17 of the 33 items are shared with at least one other country, for example Pico da Neblina (Brazil, Venezuela), so their figures describe the whole feature, not only the part inside Brazil.

The mountains of Brazil one by one

  • Pico da Neblina: 2,994 m (9,823 ft), world rank 499 of 1127. It belongs to the Cerro de la Neblina. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,886 m. It lies at 0.81° north, 66.01° west.
  • Pico da Bandeira: 2,891 m (9,486 ft), world rank 520 of 1127. It belongs to the Caparaó Mountains. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,641 m. It lies at 20.44° south, 41.80° west.
  • Mount Roraima: 2,810 m (9,219 ft), world rank 534 of 1127. It belongs to the Pakaraima Mountains. Its prominence, the climb from the lowest saddle that links it to a higher peak, is given as 2,338 m. It lies at 5.14° north, 60.76° west.
  • Corcovado: 710 m (2,329 ft), world rank 1016 of 1127. It lies at 22.95° south, 43.21° west.
  • Sugarloaf Mountain: 395 m (1,296 ft), world rank 1077 of 1127. It lies at 22.95° south, 43.16° west.

The tallest and the lowest of the 5 mountains listed for Brazil differ by 2,599 m. None reaches 5,000 m.

The lakes of Brazil one by one

  • Lagoa dos Patos: about 10,000 km² (3,861 square miles), world rank 19 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 7 m.
  • Lagoa Mirim: about 3,994 km² (1,542 square miles), world rank 62 of 568. Its greatest depth is recorded as 12 m. It is shared with Uruguay.

The rivers of Brazil one by one

  • Amazon River: about 6,800 km (4,225 miles), world rank 1 of 776. It ends at Atlantic Ocean. It is shared with Colombia, Peru, so only part of this length lies in Brazil.
  • Paraná River: about 4,880 km (3,032 miles), world rank 5 of 776. It ends at Río de la Plata. It is shared with Argentina, Paraguay, so only part of this length lies in Brazil.
  • Juruá River: about 3,100 km (1,926 miles), world rank 18 of 776. It ends at Amazon River. It is shared with Peru, so only part of this length lies in Brazil.
  • Purus River: about 2,960 km (1,839 miles), world rank 22 of 776. It ends at Solimões.
  • Japurá River: about 2,820 km (1,752 miles), world rank 26 of 776. It ends at Amazon River. It is shared with Colombia, so only part of this length lies in Brazil.
  • São Francisco River: about 2,814 km (1,749 miles), world rank 28 of 776. It ends at Atlantic Ocean.
  • Paraguay River: about 2,621 km (1,629 miles), world rank 32 of 776. It ends at Paraná River. It is shared with Argentina, Bolivia, Paraguay, so only part of this length lies in Brazil.
  • Tocantins River: about 2,450 km (1,522 miles), world rank 38 of 776. It ends at Marajó Bay.

About these figures. Heights, areas and lengths come from Wikidata (CC0), downloaded on 14 November 2026. Measurements differ between sources, and rivers and lakes change; where Wikidata holds several values we use the middle one and say so. Treat the numbers as good estimates, not survey results.
