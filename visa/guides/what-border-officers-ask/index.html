What border officers commonly ask for

Immigration is usually quick if your papers are in order and you can answer a few simple questions. A little preparation makes it so.

The basics

Border officers decide whether to let you in. A visa, an e-visa or visa-free status gives you the right to ask to enter, not an absolute right to enter. The officer checks that your passport is valid, that your visa or facility matches your trip, that you will leave on time, and that you can look after yourself while you are there.

What they ask for differs by country and by day. The following are commonly requested, and the MEA remark for your country may list some of them. Check the destination's own immigration site for the exact list.

Documents commonly asked for

  • Passport. Valid for the period the country requires. Many countries ask for six months or more beyond your stay, and some want a minimum number of blank pages. Check before you travel.
  • Visa or e-visa approval. Printed, and saved on your phone.
  • Return or onward ticket. Proof that you plan to leave. Some countries require it for visa-free entry.
  • Proof of accommodation. A hotel booking, or the address and phone number of the host.
  • Proof of funds. Recent bank statements, a card or cash. Some countries state a daily amount; others do not.
  • Travel insurance. Some countries require medical insurance, and the MEA remark may mention it.
  • Other papers. A visa or residence permit from another country, where the facility is conditional on it. An invitation, a registration number, or a health certificate where the country asks for one.

Questions you may be asked

Officers ask simple questions: why are you visiting, how long will you stay, where will you stay, who is paying, have you been here before, what is your job. Answer truthfully, briefly and clearly. Contradictions between what you say and what your papers show cause more trouble than a short answer.

Pack a small folder

Keep a folder in your hand luggage, not in the checked bag, with printed copies of everything above, in the order an officer would ask for it. Carry a pen, since many arrival forms are still on paper. Save digital copies on your phone and in your email in case a paper copy is lost.

Know your dates

Write down the date by which you must leave, and plan your return with a margin. Overstaying even by a day can lead to fines, a ban on re-entry or trouble the next time you apply for a visa anywhere.

If you are refused

Stay calm and polite. Ask to speak to the airline representative, and ask in writing for the reason, if you can. You may be sent back on the next flight. Contact the Indian embassy or consulate for help if you feel you have been treated unfairly.

Do I need a return ticket for a visa-free trip?

Often yes. Many MEA remarks and official sites list it as a condition. Check the page for your country.

How much money should I carry?

There is no universal figure. Some countries state a daily amount; for others, officers judge from your ticket, booking and statements. Check the destination's site.
