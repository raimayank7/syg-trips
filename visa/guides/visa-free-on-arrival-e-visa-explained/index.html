Visa free, visa on arrival and e-visa explained

The three words sound similar but mean three different journeys. Know which one you are on before you book.

Why there are three lists

A visa is permission to enter a country for a stated purpose and period. Governments can give it in three ways, and the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) of India keeps a separate list of each, for Indian ordinary passport holders. When you read a country page on this site, the first thing to look at is which of the three it falls under, because it decides what you do before you leave home.

Visa free

Visa free means the country lets you in without a visa for a stated period, if you meet its entry conditions. You do not apply to anyone in advance. The period is on the MEA list, for example 30 days or 90 days, and it counts from the day you enter.

Visa free does not mean no questions. You are still checked at the border, and the officer can refuse entry if something is wrong. Many visa-free entries have conditions: a passport valid for a certain time, a return or onward ticket, proof of accommodation or money, medical insurance, registration on an arrival website, or holding a visa or residence permit from certain other countries. The MEA remark on the country page shows what it knows. Read it, and then confirm on the country's own site.

Visa on arrival

With visa on arrival you do not apply before travelling; the visa is issued when you reach the border or airport. You pay the fee there, show your documents, and receive a visa or stamp. It sounds the simplest of the three, but it is the one where preparation matters most: you need the right photographs and papers, often the fee in a particular currency or card, and you may join a long queue.

Some countries make visa on arrival available only to people who hold a visa or a residence permit from certain other countries, or who send documents in advance. A few allow it only at certain airports. The MEA remark shows these conditions when it has them.

E-visa

An e-visa is a visa that you apply for online, before you leave, on the government's own website. You upload your passport page and a photo, pay the fee by card, and wait for approval by email. You print the approval or save it on your phone and show it on arrival, together with your passport. The stay allowed and the number of entries are printed on it.

An e-visa is not a guarantee of entry, but it is the strongest form of approval short of a stamp in your passport. It removes the uncertainty of arriving without a visa, and it is usually quicker than going to an embassy. The main risk is applying through the wrong website: see how to avoid fake visa websites.

Which is best?

If a country appears under more than one list, the conditions differ: the MEA is telling you there are several routes. Choose the one that fits your documents. In general, an e-visa you hold before you fly is the safest, visa free the cheapest, and visa on arrival the one that needs the most care at the border. The final word is always the destination country's own immigration website.

What if my country is not on any list?

Then the MEA does not list a facility for Indian ordinary passport holders, and you normally need to apply for a visa at the country's embassy or visa centre in advance. This site does not cover those.

Does the period on the list include the day I arrive?

Countries count differently. Use the dates on your approval or ask the embassy, and leave a margin so you leave before the period ends.
