Weather in North Korea: 11 cities compared

North Korea is in Asia. Its capital is Pyongyang and about 26.6 million people live in the country, with Won as the currency. This page covers the 11 cities of North Korea in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Kaesong has the warmest year (average 10.6 degrees C) and Chongjin the coolest (7.6 degrees C). Wonsan is the wettest with about 1,292 mm of rain a year and Chongjin the driest with about 801 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), June is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 11 of 11.

North Korea month by month

Counting the 11 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 0 in Jan, 0 in Apr, 0 in Jul, 1 in Oct. In June every one of them is comfortable. In November to April and July to August none of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Namp'o in July, with an average high of 28.8 degrees C, while Chongjin is 5.6 degrees C cooler that month (23.2).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Kaesong and Chongjin, about 510 km apart in a straight line. Chongjin is the most northern of the cities (41.8 degrees) and Kaesong the most southern (38.0 degrees), so the group spans about 420 km from north to south. All of them use the Asia/Pyongyang time zone (UTC+9:00 in January).

North Korea compared with other countries

Across its 11 cities, North Korea has an average yearly temperature of 9.4 degrees C and about 1,020 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 76 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 26 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Asia, among 29 such countries, it is number 28 for warmth and number 13 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
