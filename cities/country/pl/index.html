Weather in Poland: 9 cities compared

Poland is in Europe. Its capital is Warsaw and about 36.4 million people live in the country, with Zloty as the currency. This page covers the 9 cities of Poland in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Wroclaw has the warmest year (average 9.1 degrees C) and Gdansk the coolest (8.5 degrees C). Krakow is the wettest with about 786 mm of rain a year and Poznan the driest with about 597 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), June is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 9 of 9.

Poland month by month

Counting the 9 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 0 in Jan, 0 in Apr, 9 in Jul, 0 in Oct. In June to August every one of them is comfortable. In October to April none of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Lublin in July, with an average high of 26.3 degrees C, while Gdansk is 5.2 degrees C cooler that month (21.1).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Szczecin and Lublin, about 600 km apart in a straight line. Gdansk is the most northern of the cities (54.4 degrees) and Krakow the most southern (50.1 degrees), so the group spans about 480 km from north to south. Heights differ too: Krakow lies about 219 metres above sea level and Gdansk about 8 metres, a gap that alone cools the higher city by roughly 1.4 degrees C. All of them use the Europe/Warsaw time zone (UTC+1:00 in January).

Poland compared with other countries

Across its 9 cities, Poland has an average yearly temperature of 8.8 degrees C and about 650 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 78 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 48 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Europe, among 14 such countries, it is number 11 for warmth and number 8 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
