Weather in South Africa: 25 cities compared

South Africa is in Africa. Its capital is Pretoria and about 64.7 million people live in the country, with Rand as the currency. This page covers the 25 cities of South Africa in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Durban has the warmest year (average 22.3 degrees C) and Botshabelo the coolest (15.7 degrees C). Pietermaritzburg is the wettest with about 1,169 mm of rain a year and Gqeberha the driest with about 436 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), March is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 25 of 25.

South Africa month by month

Counting the 25 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 8 in Jan, 25 in Apr, 4 in Jul, 20 in Oct. In March to April every one of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Bloemfontein in January, with an average high of 32.1 degrees C, while Cape Town is 10.4 degrees C cooler that month (21.7).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Cape Town and Emalahleni, about 1,370 km apart in a straight line. Soshanguve is the most northern of the cities (-25.5 degrees) and Gqeberha the most southern (-34.0 degrees), so the group spans about 940 km from north to south. Heights differ too: Johannesburg lies about 1,767 metres above sea level and Durban about 21 metres, a gap that alone cools the higher city by roughly 11.3 degrees C. All of them use the Africa/Johannesburg time zone (UTC+2:00 in January).

South Africa compared with other countries

Across its 25 cities, South Africa has an average yearly temperature of 17.9 degrees C and about 650 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 52 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 49 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Africa, among 23 such countries, it is number 22 for warmth and number 14 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
