Weather in Germany: 25 cities compared

Germany is in Europe. Its capital is Berlin and about 83.5 million people live in the country, with Euro as the currency. This page covers the 25 cities of Germany in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Frankfurt am Main has the warmest year (average 10.2 degrees C) and Munich the coolest (8.4 degrees C). Munich is the wettest with about 1,133 mm of rain a year and Berlin the driest with about 604 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), September is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 25 of 25.

Germany month by month

Counting the 25 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 0 in Jan, 0 in Apr, 24 in Jul, 0 in Oct. In September every one of them is comfortable. In October to April none of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Frankfurt am Main in July, with an average high of 26.9 degrees C, while Hamburg is 4.1 degrees C cooler that month (22.8).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Munich and Hamburg-Nord, about 620 km apart in a straight line. Hamburg-Nord is the most northern of the cities (53.6 degrees) and Munich the most southern (48.1 degrees), so the group spans about 610 km from north to south. Heights differ too: Munich lies about 524 metres above sea level and Hamburg-Mitte about 8 metres, a gap that alone cools the higher city by roughly 3.4 degrees C. All of them use the Europe/Berlin time zone (UTC+1:00 in January).

Germany compared with other countries

Across its 25 cities, Germany has an average yearly temperature of 9.5 degrees C and about 780 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 75 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 41 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Europe, among 14 such countries, it is number 9 for warmth and number 4 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
