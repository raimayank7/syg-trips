Weather in Malaysia: 34 cities compared

Malaysia is in Asia. Its capital is Kuala Lumpur and about 36.0 million people live in the country, with Ringgit as the currency. This page covers the 34 cities of Malaysia in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Miri has the warmest year (average 27.9 degrees C) and Ipoh the coolest (23.6 degrees C). Kuching is the wettest with about 3,803 mm of rain a year and Malacca the driest with about 2,236 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), February is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 5 of 34.

Malaysia month by month

Counting the 34 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 0 in Jan, 2 in Apr, 0 in Jul, 0 in Oct. No month suits half of them, so a trip across these cities has to accept some heat, cold or rain. In May to January none of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Alor Setar in March, with an average high of 30.9 degrees C, while Kota Kinabalu is 3.6 degrees C cooler that month (27.3).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Sandakan and Alor Setar, about 1,960 km apart in a straight line. Kota Bharu is the most northern of the cities (6.1 degrees) and Iskandar Puteri the most southern (1.4 degrees), so the group spans about 530 km from north to south. They are spread over 2 time zones: Asia/Kuala Lumpur, Asia/Kuching.

Malaysia compared with other countries

Across its 34 cities, Malaysia has an average yearly temperature of 26.5 degrees C and about 2,540 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 7 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 2 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Asia, among 29 such countries, it is number 4 for warmth and number 2 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
