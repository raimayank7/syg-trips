Weather in South Korea: 32 cities compared

South Korea is in Asia. Its capital is Seoul and about 51.7 million people live in the country, with Won as the currency. This page covers the 32 cities of South Korea in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Jeju City has the warmest year (average 16.2 degrees C) and Wonju the coolest (8.8 degrees C). Jeju City is the wettest with about 1,578 mm of rain a year and Pohang the driest with about 1,187 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), October is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 30 of 32.

South Korea month by month

Counting the 32 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 0 in Jan, 0 in Apr, 0 in Jul, 30 in Oct. In May and October at least half of them are comfortable, which makes it the safest stretch for a trip that visits several cities. In November to April and July to September none of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Jeonju in August, with an average high of 29.4 degrees C, while Wonju is 2.8 degrees C cooler that month (26.6).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Jeju City and Paju, about 470 km apart in a straight line. Paju is the most northern of the cities (37.8 degrees) and Jeju City the most southern (33.5 degrees), so the group spans about 470 km from north to south. All of them use the Asia/Seoul time zone (UTC+9:00 in January).

South Korea compared with other countries

Across its 32 cities, South Korea has an average yearly temperature of 11.9 degrees C and about 1,290 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 71 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 18 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Asia, among 29 such countries, it is number 27 for warmth and number 9 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
