Weather in Saudi Arabia: 13 cities compared

Saudi Arabia is in Asia. Its capital is Riyadh and about 37.0 million people live in the country, with Rial as the currency. This page covers the 13 cities of Saudi Arabia in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Jeddah has the warmest year (average 29.9 degrees C) and Tabuk the coolest (21.6 degrees C). Khamis Mushait is the wettest with about 60 mm of rain a year and Madinah the driest with about 22 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), December is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 12 of 13.

Saudi Arabia month by month

Counting the 13 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 10 in Jan, 3 in Apr, 0 in Jul, 2 in Oct. In November to March at least half of them are comfortable, which makes it the safest stretch for a trip that visits several cities. In May to September none of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Al Kharj in July, with an average high of 44.9 degrees C, while Khamis Mushait is 11.6 degrees C cooler that month (33.3).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Tabuk and Najran, about 1,440 km apart in a straight line. Tabuk is the most northern of the cities (28.4 degrees) and Najran the most southern (17.5 degrees), so the group spans about 1,210 km from north to south. Heights differ too: Khamis Mushait lies about 1,998 metres above sea level and Jeddah about 7 metres, a gap that alone cools the higher city by roughly 12.9 degrees C. All of them use the Asia/Riyadh time zone (UTC+3:00 in January).

Saudi Arabia compared with other countries

Across its 13 cities, Saudi Arabia has an average yearly temperature of 25.5 degrees C and about 30 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 20 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 83 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Asia, among 29 such countries, it is number 10 for warmth and number 29 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
