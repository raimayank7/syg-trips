Weather in Indonesia: 48 cities compared

Indonesia is in Asia. Its capital is Jakarta and about 285.7 million people live in the country, with Rupiah as the currency. This page covers the 48 cities of Indonesia in our data and compares their weather. Among them, Surabaya has the warmest year (average 28.0 degrees C) and Bandung the coolest (21.4 degrees C). Padang is the wettest with about 3,532 mm of rain a year and Kupang the driest with about 1,287 mm. By the weather rules we use (see the guide to how we pick months), August is the month when most of these cities are comfortable: 26 of 48.

Indonesia month by month

Counting the 48 cities, the number that pass the comfort rule is 0 in Jan, 1 in Apr, 18 in Jul, 7 in Oct. In August at least half of them are comfortable, which makes it the safest stretch for a trip that visits several cities. In December to March none of them is comfortable. The hottest city-month of the group is Semarang in September, with an average high of 34.0 degrees C, while Padang is 8.0 degrees C cooler that month (26.0).

How far apart the cities are

The two cities farthest from each other are Medan and Jayapura, about 4,720 km apart in a straight line. Percut is the most northern of the cities (3.6 degrees) and Kupang the most southern (-10.2 degrees), so the group spans about 1,530 km from north to south. Heights differ too: Cimahi lies about 764 metres above sea level and Pontianak about 2 metres, a gap that alone cools the higher city by roughly 5.0 degrees C. They are spread over 4 time zones: Asia/Jakarta, Asia/Jayapura, Asia/Makassar, Asia/Pontianak.

Indonesia compared with other countries

Across its 48 cities, Indonesia has an average yearly temperature of 25.9 degrees C and about 2,290 mm of rain a year. Among the 83 countries on this site with at least three cities, that puts it at number 15 for warmth (1 is warmest) and number 3 for rain (1 is wettest). Within Asia, among 29 such countries, it is number 6 for warmth and number 3 for rain. The figures are the plain average of its cities, not weighted by population.
