How we pick the most comfortable months

Every city page says which months are most comfortable. Here are the exact rules, so you can judge them and adjust for your own taste.

The rules in one place

For each city and each calendar month, we use the long-term averages from NASA POWER (see the guide to reading climate data). A month counts as comfortable for sightseeing only if all four of these are true:

MeasureRuleWhy
Average daily high18 to 31 degrees CBelow 18 is cold for a day outdoors; above 31 is tiring in the afternoon
Average daily low5 degrees C or moreColder nights mean frost risk and a cold start to the day
Rain in the month120 mm or lessAbove that, wet days are common enough to disrupt plans
Average humidity82% or lessAbove that, even moderate heat feels heavy

These are our choices, based on how most walking holidays and city breaks feel, and they are the same for every city so the lists are comparable. A keen skier, a beach lover or someone who loves the heat will want different rules.

When no month passes

Some cities never pass all four tests. A desert city can be too hot in summer and chilly at night in winter; an equatorial rainforest city can be too wet and humid all year. For those cities the page says that no month passes and names the three months that came closest. Closeness is measured by a score: the gap between the average high and 24 degrees C, plus one point for every 20 mm of rain, plus one point for every 5 humidity points above 70, plus a point for every 2 degrees by which the night low is under 8. The lowest scores win. It is a transparent tie-breaker, not a scientific comfort index.

What the rules deliberately leave out

Weather is one reason to choose a month, and often not the main one. The rules do not see:

  • Crowds and prices. The most comfortable months are usually the busiest and the dearest. A month that just misses the rules, the shoulder season, may be a better trip.
  • Festivals and events. A city can be at its best during a festival in a month the weather rules rate poorly, or be unbearable during one in a month they rate highly.
  • Wind, snow cover and daylight. A mild winter month may have only eight hours of daylight; a mild spring may have strong winds.
  • Activities. Skiing needs cold and snow; swimming and beaches need warm water; trekking needs clear skies. If your trip is about one of those, treat our list as a starting point only.
  • Seasonal closures. Some attractions, ferries and mountain roads close in winter or in the rains.

Using the rules well

Start with a month page, such as where to go in March, to shortlist cities. Open the cities on your list and look at the full month-by-month table, not only the comfortable months. Then check the sources that the weather cannot give you: the official travel information of the country for entry rules and safety, and your own calendar for school holidays and festivals.

If you disagree with a rule, you can still use the table. If you do not mind rain, ignore the rain limit and read the high and low columns; if you want the cold, look for the months at the other end of the list.

Why 24 degrees C as the ideal?

It is a simple, round midpoint of the comfortable band that most people find pleasant for walking. It is used only to rank cities that fail the strict test, never to decide whether a city passes.

Are the rules different for the southern hemisphere?

No. They use the month's own temperature, rain and humidity, so a city in the south is judged by exactly the same figures. December is summer there, and the data already show it.

Will the rules change?

If we change them we will say so here and update every page together, so that the lists stay comparable.

Check before you travel. Climate averages describe a typical year from long records (NASA POWER data, refreshed November 2026). They are not a forecast, and they do not cover visas, prices, safety or closures. Check the official sources for those and the weather forecast for your dates.
