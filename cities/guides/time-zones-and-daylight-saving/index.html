Time zones and clock changes for travellers

Every city page shows the local time, the time zone and whether the clocks change. This guide explains what those mean when you travel.

What a time zone is

Time zones are regions that keep the same clock time. Most are an hour apart, but not all: India is five and a half hours ahead of the universal reference time, Nepal is five hours forty-five minutes ahead, and a few places use other fractions. The reference is called UTC, Coordinated Universal Time. A city page shows its zone as an offset such as UTC+5:30 so that you can work out the gap to your own city by subtracting.

The names on the pages

You will see names like Asia/Kolkata or Europe/Lisbon. These come from the IANA time zone database, which is what phones and computers use. The name is a place, not a country, because the rules of each place are recorded separately. Two cities in the same country can have different zone names if their clock history differs.

Daylight saving time

Some countries move their clocks forward for part of the year, usually in spring, and back in autumn, to shift daylight later into the evening. Many countries near the equator never do, because day length hardly changes there. Others have stopped. India does not change its clocks. Most of Europe and North America do, but on different dates, which causes short periods when the gap between two cities is an hour different from usual.

Each city page tells you the offset in January and in July of 2027 and says whether they differ. If they do, the city changes its clocks. The page does not give the change dates, because governments can alter them; check an official source or your phone's world clock for your dates.

Tips for crossing zones

  1. Check the gap, in both directions, for the day you travel. When one side changes its clocks and the other does not, the gap shifts by an hour.
  2. Book with local times in mind. Flight and train tickets are in the local time of each station; the arrival time already includes the zone change.
  3. Schedule calls with a margin. If you will be on a trip while working with people at home, use a clock that shows both cities.
  4. Give yourself a day. Many people find that each hour of difference takes about a day to settle. Light and sleep matter more than anything you can buy.

Day length and why it matters

The same city page also shows day length near the solstices. Near the equator the day is close to twelve hours all year. At higher latitudes the summer day can be sixteen hours or more and the winter day under eight. That changes how much of a day you have for sightseeing, and in the far north and south the sun may not set or rise at all for weeks. The figures use the standard solar formula from the city's latitude, accurate to a few minutes, and ignore local mountains.

Does the local time on the page use my phone?

The page asks your browser for the current time in the city's zone, so it is right as long as your device clock is right.

Will clock change dates be on the page?

No. They depend on laws that can change, so they are not stated. Check an official or a trustworthy clock source.

Check before you travel. Climate averages describe a typical year from long records (NASA POWER data, refreshed November 2026). They are not a forecast, and they do not cover visas, prices, safety or closures. Check the official sources for those and the weather forecast for your dates.
