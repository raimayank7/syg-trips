    What each city page tells you

    Every city page has the same plain layout so you can compare places quickly. It starts with a few facts: country, population, position, height above sea level and time zone, with the local time now. Then comes a chart and a table of the average daily high and low, the mean temperature, rainfall, humidity and sunshine for each of the twelve months. Below that are short sentences worked out from those numbers: what kind of climate the city has, when it rains, how cold the nights get, which months are most comfortable for sightseeing and what to pack.

    The numbers come from NASA POWER, a public dataset that averages weather over many years on a grid of about 50 kilometres. That makes the figures consistent from one city to another, which is what you need to compare, but a mountain or coastal city can differ from the grid around it. The guides explain this, and how we choose the comfortable months, in detail.

    Check before you travel. Climate averages describe a typical year from long records (NASA POWER data, refreshed November 2026). They are not a forecast, and they do not cover visas, prices, safety or closures. Check the official sources for those and the weather forecast for your dates.
