*Sunshine here is the solar energy reaching the ground in kWh per square metre per day: higher means clearer, sunnier days. High and low are estimates: the mean temperature of the month plus and minus half the average gap between the highest and lowest temperature of a day (NASA POWER). Rain is the total for the month.

Palmas has a hot, wet climate in this data: the yearly average is 26.4 degrees C and about 1,511 mm of rain falls over the year. September is the hottest month, with days reaching about 36.5 degrees C on average, and July is the coolest, with nights near 18.5 degrees C. The seasons change little: the warmest and coolest months differ by only 4.7 degrees C in their average temperature. Rain is concentrated: December to March bring about 63% of the year's rain, while July is the driest month with about 1 mm. Daytime highs average 35 degrees C or more in September, which is hard going for walking tours in the afternoon. January to April feel muggy: the air is warm and the average humidity is 80% or higher.

Averages hide the extremes. In this dataset (hourly model values for 2001 to 2025 over the grid cell around the city) the hottest reading was about 42.6 degrees C, in October, and the coldest about 14.1 degrees C, in June. Real thermometers in a street can read a few degrees more extreme.

Best time to visit Palmas

Judged by weather alone, the most comfortable months for sightseeing in Palmas are May to June. In those months the average high is between 18 and 31 degrees C, nights stay above 5 degrees C, rainfall is 120 mm or less and the air is not very humid. That is a narrow window, so book early if you travel then, and check the weather forecast close to your dates. This only measures weather. Crowds, prices, school holidays, festivals and closures also decide the best time, so look at those too.

Rule used, the same for every city: a month counts as comfortable when the average high is 18 to 31 degrees C, the average night low is at least 5, the rain is 120 mm or less and the average humidity is 82% or less. It is a rule, not an opinion.

What to pack

From the monthly numbers, plan to take an umbrella or a light rain jacket for December to March; light cotton clothes, a hat, sunscreen and a water bottle for September. A packing list for your kind of trip is on the trips site: packing lists.

Day length and sunshine

Day length changes with the season. Around 21 June the sun is up for about 11 hours 32 minutes, and around 21 December for about 12 hours 43 minutes (standard solar formula, accurate to a few minutes, from the city's latitude of -10.2 degrees).

How much rain does Palmas get?

About 1,511 mm a year in this data. January is the wettest month (about 268 mm) and July the driest (about 1 mm).

How hot does Palmas get?

The hottest month is September, when the average daily high is about 36.5 degrees C and the average night-time low about 22.4 degrees C. Individual days can be hotter than the average.

What time zone is Palmas in?

Palmas uses the America/Araguaina time zone, which is UTC-3:00 in January and the same in July (no clock change).
