International trip packing list

For a foreign trip, the important packing is done at a desk, not at the suitcase: passport, visa, insurance, money and bookings. Clothes can be bought anywhere; a missing document can end the trip at the airport.

Tips for this kind of trip

  • Many countries require a passport to be valid for some months beyond the visit, commonly six. Check the destination's official immigration website, and renew early if it is close.
  • Entry rules, visa types and fees change. Rely only on the embassy's or government's official website, not on agents' summaries.
  • Airlines set their own baggage allowances and rules for liquids, batteries and power banks. Read your airline's page before packing; power banks normally go in cabin baggage only.
  • Tell your bank you are travelling, and switch on international use for the card. Note the bank's number for reporting a lost card from abroad.
  • Some medicines that are ordinary in India are restricted elsewhere. Carry prescriptions, and check the destination's rules for anything stronger than common tablets.
  • Customs rules limit what you may take out and bring back, including cash, gold and food. Read the official customs guide for travellers.
  • Keep passport, cards and some cash on your body in a neck or waist pouch on travel days. Leave a copy of your itinerary with family.
  • Note the address and phone number of the Indian embassy or consulate where you are going.

Questions

How much validity should my passport have?

It depends on the country. Many ask for at least six months beyond your stay. Check the destination's official immigration or embassy website.

Can I carry medicines abroad?

Usually yes, in original packing with a prescription, in quantities for personal use. Some countries restrict certain medicines, so check the destination's official rules.
