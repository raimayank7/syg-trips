Before you leave home: the going-away checklist

The best trips begin with a boring hour of checking. This list covers what to arrange in the week before, and what to switch off, lock and carry on the day.

A week before

Bookings and papers

  • Recheck every booking: dates, names as on identity cards, timings, terminal or station.
  • Save tickets, hotel confirmations and identity proofs offline on your phone, and carry one printed set.
  • Check the validity of identity cards, driving licence and, for foreign travel, passport and visa.
  • Note the cancellation deadlines of bookings you might change.

Money

  • Tell your bank if you are going abroad, and switch on international use for the card you will carry.
  • Carry two ways to pay, kept separately, and some cash.
  • Check the daily limits on your cards and UPI.
  • Pay the bills that fall due while you are away: electricity, phone, credit card, EMIs, school fees.

Health

  • Refill regular medicines. Carry prescriptions.
  • See the doctor if a condition is unsettled, and before hill or foreign travel with elders or small children.
  • Put together the first aid pouch.

Phone

  • Download offline maps of the destination, and tickets and boarding passes.
  • Check the mobile plan: enough data, and a roaming pack if needed.
  • Back up photos and contacts.
  • Turn on Find My Device or Find My, and note the phone's IMEI (dial *#06#).
  • Charge the power bank.

Vehicle, for a road trip: service, tyres, papers. See the road trip checklist.

Two days before

  • Laundry done; start the packing pile. Use the packing list maker.
  • Fridge: stop buying fresh food; use or give away what will spoil.
  • Deliveries: pause the newspaper, milk and any regular parcels. A pile at the door announces an empty house.
  • Plants and pets: arrange watering and care. Leave written instructions and the vet's number.
  • Tell one neighbour or relative you trust: your dates, a contact number, and where a spare key is. Ask them to glance at the house.
  • Do not announce the trip publicly on social media before or during it. Post the photographs when you are back.
  • Valuables: move jewellery and important papers to a locker or a relative's home for long absences.
  • Confirm the taxi or the lift to the station.

The last hour at home

Walk through the house room by room, in the same order every time.

Kitchen

  • Gas cylinder regulator off; piped gas valve closed.
  • Hobs, oven, toaster, mixer and water purifier unplugged.
  • Dustbin emptied; no dirty dishes; counters wiped.
  • Fridge: either emptied, switched off and left open, for long trips, or left running with nothing that will rot.

Water

  • All taps closed tight. Main valve or the motor switched off.
  • Washing machine inlet tap closed.
  • Geyser switched off.

Power

  • Iron, heater, chargers, television and computer unplugged.
  • Switch off at the board everything except what must run: fridge, an inverter, a security camera, an aquarium pump.
  • A lamp on a timer makes a house look lived in.

Doors and windows

  • Every window latched; balcony and terrace doors bolted.
  • Curtains partly drawn, as on a normal day.
  • Main door locked and tried. If you use a lock that latches by itself, keys in hand first.

In your hand

  • Tickets, identity cards, money, cards.
  • Phone, charger, power bank.
  • Medicines.
  • Keys, with a spare left with the trusted neighbour.
  • Spectacles.
  • Water and something to eat.

Say the six aloud at the door: tickets, identity, money, phone, medicines, keys.

For longer absences

  • Clear the fridge and switch it off with the door propped open.
  • Cover furniture and beds with old sheets against dust.
  • Disconnect car and inverter batteries as their makers advise, or ask someone to run the car weekly.
  • Give your building's office or security your dates and a contact.
  • Redirect or hold post.
  • Check that home insurance, if you have it, does not need notice of a long absence.

When you come back

Open windows, run taps for a minute, check the gas before lighting, restart the fridge and give it a few hours before stocking it, and take meter readings if a bill looks odd later. Then unpack the same day: the bag you leave for a week is the bag that still holds the charger you think you lost.

What is the most common thing people forget before leaving?

Switching off the geyser and the gas, pausing the milk and newspaper, and carrying medicines and phone chargers.

Should I switch off the fridge when travelling?

For a few days, leave it on with nothing perishable inside. For two weeks or more, empty it, switch it off and leave the door open to prevent smell and mould.
