First time flying: what happens at the airport, step by step

An airport looks confusing, but every flight follows the same six steps in the same order. Once you know them, the rest is reading signboards.

Before the day

  1. Read your ticket. Note the airline, flight number, date, departure time, terminal and the name exactly as on your identity proof. Large cities have more than one terminal, some far apart.
  2. Read your airline's baggage page. It states how many bags you may carry into the cabin and check in, the weight and size of each, and the charge for extra. These limits differ by airline and by ticket type, so check your own.
  3. Check what cannot be carried. Airlines and airport security publish lists. As a general pattern everywhere: sharp objects, lighters and large containers of liquid may not go in cabin bags; power banks and spare lithium batteries may not go in checked bags. Your airline's page has the exact rules.
  4. Web check-in. Most airlines open check-in on their website or app a day or two before departure. You choose a seat and get a boarding pass on your phone. It saves a queue.
  5. Carry original photo identity for every traveller, including children. For flights within India, a government photo identity card; for foreign travel, the passport and visa. See international trip.

How early to reach

Airlines state when their check-in counters close, usually well before departure, and they close on time. As a general habit, plan to be at the airport about two hours before a domestic flight and three hours before an international one, and earlier on holidays and at big airports. Your airline's message or website gives its own advice; follow that.

At the airport: six steps

1. Entry. At the terminal door, show your ticket (on phone or paper) and identity card to the security staff.

2. Check-in and baggage drop. Find your airline's counters on the display boards. If you have checked in online and have only cabin baggage, skip this and go to security. Otherwise: - give your identity card and booking reference; - put the bag to be checked on the belt to be weighed and tagged; - receive your boarding pass and a baggage tag receipt. Keep the receipt until you have your bag back.

Many airports have self-service kiosks that print the boarding pass and bag tag.

3. Security check. Join the queue with your boarding pass. Put your cabin bag, phone, wallet, belt, laptop and other electronics, and any liquids as directed, in a tray on the belt. Walk through the frame and wait to be checked. Collect everything from the tray; people often leave phones and laptops behind here.

4. Find the gate. Your boarding pass shows a gate number and boarding time. Gates can change: check the display screens, not only the pass. Walk to the gate first, see how far it is, then use washrooms or shops nearby. Boarding closes some minutes before departure.

5. Boarding. When your flight or row is called, queue with boarding pass and identity card. You walk through a bridge or take a bus to the aircraft.

6. On board. Find your seat by the number above the row (letters A to F mark the seat). Put the larger bag in the overhead locker and a small one under the seat in front. Sit, fasten the seat belt, and put the phone in flight mode when asked.

During the flight

  • Watch the safety demonstration, even if others do not.
  • Keep the belt loosely fastened whenever seated.
  • Ears may feel blocked as the aircraft climbs and descends. Swallowing, yawning, chewing or sipping water helps. Babies do well feeding at those times.
  • Press the call button above you if you need the crew. Water is available on request.
  • Use the washroom when the seat-belt sign is off.
  • Mild bumps, called turbulence, are normal.

After landing

  1. Stay seated until the aircraft stops and the sign goes off.
  2. Follow the signs for Arrivals and Baggage claim.
  3. A screen shows which belt has your flight's bags. Check the tag number against your receipt before taking a bag; many look alike.
  4. If your bag does not arrive or is damaged, report it at the airline's baggage desk before leaving the hall, and get a written report.
  5. For onward transport, use the official prepaid taxi counter, app cabs from the marked pick-up area, or the airport bus or metro. Ignore touts inside the terminal.

For an international arrival, immigration comes before baggage claim and customs after it.

Small things that help

  • Wear shoes that slip off easily and avoid metal-heavy belts and jewellery, to pass security quickly.
  • Carry medicines, valuables, chargers and one change of clothes in the cabin bag.
  • Keep an empty bottle to fill after security, where water points are available.
  • Charge your phone; the boarding pass may be on it. A paper copy is a wise backup.
  • If you need help walking or are travelling with an elder, request wheelchair assistance from the airline in advance.
  • If anything is unclear, ask the airline's staff or the airport help desk. Asking is normal.
Do I need a printout of my ticket?

A ticket or boarding pass on the phone is accepted at most airports. A printout is a sensible backup in case the phone battery dies.

What happens if I miss my flight?

The ticket's rules decide. Go to the airline's counter at once; depending on the fare, you may be moved to a later flight for a fee, or the ticket may be lost.

Can I carry food on a flight?

Dry and solid home food is generally allowed in cabin baggage on domestic flights; liquids and gravies fall under the liquids rules. Check your airline's and the airport's guidance.
