Hill station and winter trip packing list

In the hills the temperature drops quickly after sunset and climbs again in the midday sun, so the answer is layers, not one heavy coat. Three thin layers that can be added and removed keep you warmer and pack smaller than one bulky jacket.

Tips for this kind of trip

  • Wear the bulky jacket and shoes on the journey rather than packing them.
  • Mountain weather turns within an hour. Keep the windproof layer and a cap in the day bag even on a sunny morning.
  • On winding roads, sit in front, look at the road ahead and avoid reading or scrolling. Eat lightly before the drive.
  • At high altitude, take the first day slowly, drink water and avoid alcohol. Headache, nausea or breathlessness at rest are warnings: do not climb higher, and see a doctor if they persist. Anyone with a heart or lung condition should ask their doctor before the trip.
  • Carry cash. Card machines and mobile networks are unreliable in the hills.
  • Check road and weather news before setting out in winter and in the monsoon; landslides and snow close roads without notice.
  • Hotel rooms in the hills may have no heating. Ask when booking, and carry warm nightwear.

Questions

How many layers do I need for a hill station?

Three: a thermal or cotton inner layer, a fleece or sweater in the middle, and a windproof outer jacket. Add a cap, gloves and warm socks.

Do I need sunscreen in the mountains?

Yes. Sunlight is stronger at height and reflects off snow, so skin and lips burn faster than on the plains.
