Packing list maker

Choose the kind of trip and the number of nights. A checklist appears with clothing counts worked out, ready to tick, print or send.

How it works

Every trip needs the same core: documents and money, clothes, toiletries, health items and the phone kit. The maker starts from that core, then adds the section for your kind of trip: swimwear and sun protection for a beach, layers for the hills, the car checklist for a road trip, and so on. Tick the boxes for a baby, rain or cold to add those sections to any trip.

The number of nights sets the clothing counts: a top for each day up to a week, a pair of bottoms for every two to three days, and a set of underwear and socks for each day plus one spare. Beyond a week the counts stop rising, because it makes more sense to wash clothes than to carry a fortnight's worth.

Your ticks are remembered in this browser, so you can pack over several evenings. The Copy and WhatsApp buttons send the whole list as plain text, which is handy for dividing the packing among family members.

Make the list your own

No general list knows your trip. Before you pack, read down it once and:

  • cross out what does not apply to you;
  • add what only you would know: a gift for the host, the child's school project, the documents for the purpose of the trip, your instrument or sports kit;
  • mark who carries what, so that two people do not both bring the first aid kit and nobody brings the chargers.

How to pack well

  1. Start two or three days ahead. Put a bag or box in a corner and drop things in as you think of them. Last-night packing is how chargers and medicines get forgotten.
  2. Lay everything out before it goes in. Seeing it all at once shows what is missing and what is too much.
  3. Choose clothes that all go together. Two or three colours, so that every top works with every bottom.
  4. Wear the bulkiest things on the journey: the jacket, the heavy shoes.
  5. Roll soft clothes; fold stiff ones. Put shoes in bags at the bottom, near the wheels of a suitcase.
  6. Liquids in a zip pouch, in case one leaks.
  7. Keep a day bag with everything you cannot afford to lose or be without for a day: documents, money, medicines, phone, charger, a change of clothes, and for children, food and nappies.
  8. Never put medicines, documents, keys, cash or valuables in checked or stowed luggage.
  9. Leave a little space. You will bring something back.

Bag rules differ: check your own

Airlines, and some bus and train operators, limit the size and weight of bags and what may go in them, and the limits differ between operators and ticket types. Batteries and power banks have their own rules on flights. We do not quote these limits because they change; read the baggage page of your own airline or operator before you pack.

Can I save my list?

Your choices and ticks are kept in this browser on this device. To keep a permanent copy, use Copy, WhatsApp or Print.

How many clothes should I pack for a week?

About seven tops, three or four bottoms and eight sets of underwear and socks, fewer if you can wash clothes on the way.

Why does the list not grow after seven nights?

For longer trips it is easier to wash or launder clothes once a week than to carry and manage more.
