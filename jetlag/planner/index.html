Jet lag planner

Pick where you fly from and to, and the day you fly.

How the plan is worked out

The planner reads the clock offsets of the two cities on the date you pick, so clock changes are included. It then uses the average adaptation rates in the CDC Yellow Book: about 1 hour a day after eastward travel and about 1.5 hours a day after westward travel. It shows how many days that suggests, and a bedtime schedule that moves your sleep by one hour a day for up to three days before you fly, as the CDC suggests, followed by the local bedtime to aim for.

These are averages, not promises, and the planner gives general wellness guidance only. It does not suggest medicines or doses; if you wish to use melatonin or any medicine, ask a doctor. Our guides explain the reasons behind each step.

Wellness information, not medical advice. The plans here follow general guidance from the CDC Yellow Book (2026 edition, page dated 23 April 2025) and the NHS (page last reviewed 31 May 2023). If you have a medical condition, take regular medicines, or are considering melatonin or sleeping tablets, talk to a doctor before you travel.
