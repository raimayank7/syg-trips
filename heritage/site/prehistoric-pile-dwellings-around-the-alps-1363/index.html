Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps

Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps is a World Heritage site in Italy, France, Austria, Slovenia, Switzerland and Germany, inscribed in 2011. UNESCO lists it as a cultural site, valued for what people built or created. It is a transboundary site, shared between more than one country. The listing covers 111 separate parts (components), so the area you visit may be only one of them. UNESCO gives its size as about 274 hectares (2.7 square kilometres).

Why it is on the list

A site is added to the World Heritage List when it meets at least one of ten criteria. Prehistoric Pile Dwellings around the Alps meets criteria iv and v: (iv) an outstanding example of a type of building, architecture or landscape; (v) an outstanding example of a traditional way of settling or using land or sea.

These are our own plain-words summaries of UNESCO's criteria numbers. The full wording and UNESCO's own description of this site are on its website.

When to visit

The nearest city in our weather data is Zuerich, about 59 km away. Its climate is cool and wet: the yearly average is 9.0 degrees C with about 1,311 mm of rain. By our weather rules the most comfortable months for sightseeing there are September. Wettest month: August (about 146 mm). Hottest month: July (highs near 23.2 degrees C). Coldest: January (nights near -3.7 degrees C).

Height, coast and local weather can make a site very different from the nearest city: use this as a guide, not a forecast.
