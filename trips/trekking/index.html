Trekking packing list

On a trek you carry everything on your back, so each item must earn its place. The rule of experienced trekkers is simple: pack for warmth, dryness, water and feet, and leave out anything that is only for comfort.

Tips for this kind of trip

  • Keep the pack as light as you can. Lay out everything, then remove a third.
  • Never trek in new shoes. Wear them for two or three weeks beforehand.
  • Pack clothes inside plastic bags within the rucksack, heavy items close to your back and things needed often (water, rain jacket, snacks) on top or in side pockets.
  • Tell someone at home your route and when you expect to be back. In remote areas go with a registered guide or organiser, and carry any permit the forest department requires.
  • Start early and turn back by the time you set, summit or not. Weather in the mountains worsens in the afternoon.
  • Drink small amounts often. Climb slowly at altitude; headache, nausea, dizziness or breathlessness at rest mean stop ascending, and descend if they do not ease.
  • Carry out everything you carry in. Leave no plastic on the trail.

Questions

How heavy should my trekking bag be?

As light as possible. Many trekkers aim to carry no more than about a fifth of their body weight, and less on steep or long treks.

Can I trek in sports shoes?

For short, dry, easy trails, sturdy sports shoes with good grip will do. For rocky, wet, snowy or multi-day treks, use proper trekking shoes with ankle support.
