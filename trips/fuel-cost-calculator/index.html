Fuel cost calculator for a road trip

Type the distance, your vehicle's mileage and the fuel price you pay. The page works out the fuel cost and each traveller's share.

The formula

Fuel needed (litres) = distance ÷ mileage

Fuel cost = litres × price per litre

A 600 km round trip in a car that gives 15 km to the litre needs 600 ÷ 15 = 40 litres. Multiply 40 by the price you pay per litre for the fuel cost. Add tolls and parking, divide by the number of people sharing, and you have each person's part.

The page needs three figures from you. None is filled in for you, because each depends on your vehicle and your city.

Finding your three figures

Distance. Use a maps app for the route you will actually drive, not the straight-line distance. Add the running about at the destination: sightseeing, detours and the search for parking easily add a tenth or more.

Mileage. Use what your vehicle really gives, not the figure in the brochure. To measure it:

  1. Fill the tank to the brim and set the trip meter to zero.
  2. Drive as usual until the tank is at least half used.
  3. Fill to the brim again and note the litres put in.
  4. Mileage = kilometres on the trip meter ÷ litres filled.

Highway mileage is usually better than city mileage. On a trip it falls with a full load of people and luggage, a roof carrier, air conditioning, hill roads and high speed.

Fuel price. Petrol and diesel prices differ from state to state and city to city, and are revised by the oil companies. Use the price shown at your own pump, or on your oil company's official app or website for the city where you will fill up.

Tolls and other costs

National highways and expressways charge tolls, paid through FASTag, and the amount depends on the stretch, the kind of vehicle and whether you return within the day. Maps apps show an estimate for a route, and the highway authority publishes the rates. Add parking fees, any permit or entry charge for vehicles, and the driver's allowance if you have hired one.

Sharing the cost fairly

  • Sharing fuel and tolls equally among everyone in the car, driver included, is the usual way among friends.
  • If one person's car is used, the others sometimes cover a little more than the fuel, since wear, tyres and servicing are real costs that the owner bears. Agree it beforehand.
  • For trips with several other expenses, put the fuel into the cost splitter as one expense paid by whoever filled the tank.

Using less fuel on the trip

  • Check tyre pressure before leaving. Soft tyres waste fuel and wear faster.
  • Drive at a steady, moderate speed. Fuel use climbs steeply at high speed.
  • Accelerate gently and look ahead, so that you brake less.
  • Remove a roof carrier and unneeded weight.
  • Switch off the engine at long halts.
  • Start early to avoid crawling through city traffic.

Electric and CNG vehicles

The same idea works with other units. For CNG, use kilometres per kilogram and the price per kilogram. For an electric vehicle, use kilometres per unit (kWh) and the price per unit you pay for charging; public fast chargers usually cost more per unit than charging at home.

How do I calculate fuel cost for a trip?

Divide the distance by your vehicle's mileage to get litres, then multiply by the price per litre.

Why is my real fuel cost higher than the calculation?

Mileage on a loaded car with the air conditioner on, in hills or traffic, is lower than normal, and the distance driven is always more than the map distance.

Does the calculator know today's petrol price?

No. Prices differ by city and change often, so you enter the price at your pump.
