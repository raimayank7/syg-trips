Trip budget planner

Type your own estimates under each head. The planner adds them up and shows the cost for each person and for each day.

For each line, type an amount in rupees and choose how it is counted.

Why the planner has no prices in it

Fares, room rates and ticket prices change with the season, the day of the week, how early you book and where you are going. Any figure printed here would be wrong for most readers within weeks. So the planner gives you the structure and does the arithmetic, and the numbers are yours: taken from the booking sites, the operator's own page, or a phone call to the hotel, on the day you plan.

How to use it

  1. Enter the number of people and days.
  2. For each head, type an amount and choose how it is counted: - for the whole group, whole trip: one sum, such as a hired vehicle; - per person: such as a train or flight ticket each; - per day, whole group: such as two hotel rooms a night; - per person per day: such as food.
  3. The total, the share for each person, and the cost per day appear below, with each head's part of the whole as a percentage.

Your figures are saved in this browser, so you can return and adjust them as bookings are made.

Where to find realistic figures

  • Travel: the railway's official website or app, the airline's own site, the state transport corporation's site. Search the actual dates.
  • Stay: the hotel's own site or a phone call, and a booking site to compare. Check what is included: breakfast, taxes, extra bed.
  • Food: ask someone who has been recently, or look at menus that restaurants publish. Then add a margin: people eat out more on holiday than they plan to.
  • Local transport: ask the hotel what an auto, taxi or hired car for the day usually costs there.
  • Entry tickets: the official website of the monument, park or temple trust. Many places charge more for cameras or on weekends.

The heads people forget

  • Getting to and from the station or airport at both ends.
  • Taxes and service charges on rooms and meals.
  • Tips, porters, parking, tolls.
  • Snacks, water, tea and coffee: small, but many times a day for a group.
  • Activities that are the reason for going: the safari, the boat, the ropeway, the guide.
  • Mobile data or roaming.
  • Travel insurance for trips abroad.
  • Things to buy before leaving: a bag, warm clothes, medicines.
  • Leaving pets or elders cared for at home.

Keep a reserve

Add a line for the unexpected. A common habit is to hold back about a tenth of the total: a missed connection, a medical visit, a night extended by weather. On a trip abroad or to remote hills, hold more. If you do not use it, it is the start of the next trip's fund.

Ways to bring the total down

  • Dates matter most. Weekdays and the weeks just outside school holidays and long weekends cost far less. Our sister site lists the long weekends to plan around, or to avoid.
  • Book travel early; book stay with free cancellation and look again closer to the date.
  • Share: a hired vehicle or a homestay split among many is cheaper per head than separate tickets and rooms.
  • Stay near what you came for. A cheaper room far away is paid for in transport and time.
  • Carry breakfast things and a water bottle.
  • Choose two or three paid activities, not all of them.

From budget to settlement

Before the trip, this planner tells everyone what to expect and whether the plan needs trimming. During and after it, the cost splitter records who actually paid what and works out who owes whom.

Does the planner suggest prices?

No. Prices vary too much by place, date and choice. It adds up the figures you enter.

How much should I keep for emergencies?

Many travellers keep about ten per cent of the trip's cost in reserve, more for foreign or remote travel.

Is the budget saved?

Yes, in this browser on this device only. Use Copy or WhatsApp to share it.
