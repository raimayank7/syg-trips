Trip cost splitter: who owes whom

Add the people, then each expense and who paid it. The page shows what everyone owes and the fewest payments that settle the whole trip.

    How to use it

    1. Add everyone on the trip. Type a name and tap Add, or type several names with commas between them.
    2. Add each expense: what it was for, the amount, and who paid. By default everyone shares it. If an expense was only for some people, such as a ride that three of five took, untick the others.
    3. Read the result. The table shows what each person paid, what their fair share was, and the balance. Below it is the short list of payments that settles everything.
    4. Send it to the group with the WhatsApp button, or copy or print it.

    The trip is saved in this browser as you type, so you can add expenses day by day through the holiday and settle at the end. Nothing is uploaded; if you clear your browser data or change phones, it is gone, so share the summary when you finish.

    How the sums are done

    For each expense, the amount is divided equally among the people ticked for it. Each person's share is the total of those parts. Their balance is what they paid minus their share: a positive balance means the group owes them, a negative one means they owe.

    Then the page pairs the person who owes the most with the person who is owed the most, and repeats until every balance is zero. This gives a short list: with five people, at most four payments are ever needed, however many expenses there were.

    All arithmetic is done in whole paise, and when an amount does not divide evenly, the odd paise are spread one each among the sharers, so that the shares always add up exactly to what was spent.

    A worked example

    Asha, Bala and Chitra go away for a weekend.

    ExpenseAmountPaid byShared by
    Hotel₹6,000AshaEveryone
    Taxi₹1,500BalaEveryone
    Boat ride₹900ChitraAsha and Chitra

    Shares: the hotel is ₹2,000 each and the taxi ₹500 each. The boat is ₹450 each for Asha and Chitra only. So Asha's share is ₹2,950, Bala's ₹2,500 and Chitra's ₹2,950.

    Balances: Asha paid ₹6,000, so she gets back ₹3,050. Bala paid ₹1,500, so he owes ₹1,000. Chitra paid ₹900, so she owes ₹2,050.

    Settlement: Chitra pays Asha ₹2,050 and Bala pays Asha ₹1,000. Two payments, and everyone is square. Enter the same figures above to see it.

    Tips for smooth sharing

    • Agree the rules before the trip, not at the end: what is shared, what is personal, and how unequal things such as alcohol or single rooms are handled. See splitting costs fairly.
    • Enter expenses the same day, while everyone remembers. Keep bills, or a photo of them.
    • One expense per bill. If a restaurant bill covers a shared meal and one person's extra dish, enter the extra as a separate expense shared by that person alone.
    • Couples and families can be entered as one "person" if they will settle as one, but then each expense is split per unit, not per head. For a per-head split, enter each person.
    • Advances and kitty money: if everyone puts money into a common fund, enter each expense as paid by whoever holds the fund, and each contribution as an expense paid by the contributor and "shared" only by the fund holder.

    Settling up

    UPI makes the final payments easy, but check the name shown before you pay, and never scan a QR code or approve a request to *receive* money: receiving needs no action from you. Our sister site explains the UPI request trick.

    Does the splitter handle unequal shares?

    It splits each expense equally among the people ticked for that expense. For unequal amounts, enter the expense in parts: one part shared by all, another by the person who had more.

    Is my data sent anywhere?

    No. The sums are done in your browser, and the trip is stored only on your own device.

    Can several people add expenses to the same trip?

    Not at the same time; the page works on one device. One person keeps the record and shares the summary with the group.
