How to read the ASI list of monuments

The official list is long and plain. A few tips make it much easier to use.

How the list is organised

The ASI list is arranged first by state or union territory, and within each by circle. A circle is a regional office of the ASI that looks after the monuments in an area; a large state may have several circles, a small one may share a circle with its neighbours. For each monument the list gives a serial number, a name, a locality and a district. That is all. On this site each state has its own page, the districts are folded out inside it, and the biggest districts have a page of their own.

The name

The name is the ASI's own wording and it is often a description rather than a title. You will see names such as "Temple of Vishnu", "Old fort", "Group of tombs", "Remains of ancient site" or "Tomb and mosque of" a person. Some name a single building and some a whole group, so the number of entries is not a count of buildings. A single entry may be a large complex with many structures, while another is one stone. Spellings differ from modern ones and from other sources: Kos Minar may appear as Kosminar, a masjid as a mosque, and old place names remain as they were recorded.

The locality and the district

The locality is the town, village or area where the monument stands, as recorded by the ASI. The district is the district at the time of recording. Districts are sometimes split or renamed, so a monument may now lie in a newer district than the one the list names. If you cannot find a place under the district you expect, try the neighbouring districts or search by the locality or name instead.

Using the search

The search box on the home page looks in the name, locality and district at once. Type at least three letters. Try a part of a name ("stepwell", "Kos Minar", "rock") or a village. Because spellings vary, try alternatives if you get nothing: "masjid" and "mosque", "baoli" and "stepwell", "Siva" and "Shiva". On a state page, the filter box narrows the districts to those that contain your word.

The groupings by type

We group the entries by words in their names: temples, forts, tombs, mosques, stepwells and tanks, caves and rock-cut sites, pillars, palaces, ancient sites and mounds, inscriptions, and churches and colonial buildings. This is our own guide, made by matching words, and not an ASI classification. A name like "Ancient site" says nothing about what is there, so many entries fit no group, and some fit more than one, like a "fort and gateway". Use the groups to browse, not to count.

Checking before you travel

Once you have found a monument, the list will not tell you whether you can visit it. Look for the ASI's own page if it is a major site, the state tourism page, and recent traveller reports. Ask locally about access. Many small entries are on private land or at the end of a rough track. If you plan a trip around several monuments, our trip planner can hold the plan, and the heritage section covers the UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Why does the same monument seem to appear twice?

Large complexes are sometimes listed as several entries, for example each gateway, tomb or temple separately, or a monument may be listed under two localities.

Why is a famous place not in the list?

It may be protected by a state, it may be a living place of worship looked after by a trust, or it may be listed under a different name. Try searching a part of its name or its locality.

Is the list complete and current?

It is the ASI's list as downloaded on 14 November 2026. It can be revised, so for a legal question rely on the current official notifications.

Check before you visit. This list is the Archaeological Survey of India's list of centrally protected monuments, as downloaded on 14 November 2026. It gives no opening times, ticket prices or access details, and it can change as monuments are added or removed. Check the ASI's own pages, the state tourism office and local sources before you go. Many monuments are small or remote and are not open to visitors in the usual way.
